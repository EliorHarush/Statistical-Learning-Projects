This assignment explores the use of resampling methods, specifically the nonparametric bootstrap, to estimate the variability of a statistical estimator. The objective was to determine the optimal weight, alpha, used to combine two variables, X and Y, such that the total variance of the resulting combination is minimized. This is a classic optimization problem where the analytical solution is derived by taking the derivative of the variance of the linear combination with respect to alpha and setting it to zero.

The primary challenge of this assignment was the 'from scratch' implementation of the bootstrap procedure. To adhere to strict academic requirements, library functions for resampling were avoided. Instead, explicit sampling from the Empirical Distribution Function (EDF) was performed. This involved generating 1,000 sets of uniform random numbers between zero and one. These numbers were then transformed into discrete indices using the floor of the product of the sample size and the random number. This method ensures that every observation in the original dataset has an equal probability of being selected with replacement in each bootstrap replicate.

Using the provided dataset of 100 observations, the plug-in estimate for the optimal weight was calculated to be approximately 0.5425. This indicates that roughly 54% of the weight should be assigned to the first variable to achieve the minimum variance. To assess the reliability of this estimate, 1,000 bootstrap simulations were conducted. The resulting distribution of alpha estimates allowed for the construction of a 95% confidence interval using the percentile method, yielding a range of [0.4086, 0.7075].

The validity of these results rests on the assumption that the observations are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.). By using a nonparametric approach, we avoided making assumptions about the normality of the underlying data, allowing the empirical distribution to serve as a direct proxy for the population. This exercise demonstrates how bootstrapping provides a robust framework for quantifying uncertainty in complex statistical estimators and highlights the relationship between raw data and theoretical optimization.
