In this project, we developed tree-based models to predict real estate sale prices and classify distressed properties using a 300-observation training subset of the Ames Housing dataset. A primary challenge was the highly imbalanced classification target, as distressed sales represented only 15 percent of the training set.

For the regression task, we built a CART decision tree from scratch utilizing greedy Sum of Squared Errors reduction, optimizing its depth through a custom 5-fold cross-validation loop. We then engineered a custom Random Forest from scratch using bootstrap aggregation and random feature subsets to decorrelate the trees and reduce variance. These custom architectures were benchmarked against a library Gradient Boosting Regressor tuned via grid search, which achieved the lowest mean squared error by sequentially minimizing bias.

For the classification task, our goal was to output calibrated probabilities and minimize cross-entropy loss. To properly address the 15 percent distressed prevalence, our imbalance-handling strategy relied on synthetic weighting. We utilized the balanced class weight parameter for the scikit-learn Random Forest and dynamically computed sample weights for the Gradient Boosting Classifier. This approach forced the models to heavily penalize misclassifications of the minority class, preventing naive predictions of the majority class.

Extracting mean decrease in impurity variables importance revealed distinct market drivers. Overall quality and living area dictated the continuous sale price, whereas property age and condition were the primary indicators of a distressed sale. Overall, the models successfully captured complex non-linear interactions while robustly navigating severe class imbalance.
