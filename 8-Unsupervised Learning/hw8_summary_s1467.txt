The raw text was preprocessed by lowercasing, removing punctuation, and filtering English stop words. To isolate true semantic signals, we represented the corpus using TF-IDF vectorization, applying document frequency boundaries of min_df=2 and max_df=0.95 to down-weight ubiquitous and highly rare terms. Because TF-IDF yields a highly sparse matrix, Truncated SVD (Latent Semantic Analysis) was applied to compress the feature space to 50 principal components, discarding background noise and avoiding the curse of dimensionality.

These reduced embeddings were clustered using K-means with twenty random initializations to ensure mathematical stability. To determine the optimal number of topics, we performed a sensitivity analysis using the Silhouette Method, evaluating cluster cohesion and separation across a search grid of two to fifteen clusters. The average Silhouette score revealed a definitive global peak at exactly eleven clusters, confidently establishing K-hat as eleven. Forcing fewer clusters artificially merged distinct concepts, while more clusters fractured cohesive groups.

Interpreting the top keywords for these fourteen clusters revealed clear thematic boundaries: October 7th (jews, killing, suffered, hamas),law (just, don, body, subject), politics (got, sky, left, rights), sports (year, won, league, standard), Middle Eastern geopolitics (Israel, arab, arabs, Israeli), airports (April, pm, Philadelphia, try), automobile market (car, cars, price, list), space exploration (shuttle, nasa, space, need), christianity (god, jesus, faith, religion), religion (law, sabbath, ceremonial, paul), sports gambling (baseball, dealer, hit, season), Middle eastern history (Turkish, turks, turkey, greeks), physics (small, growth, rate, rocket), Armenian genocide (Armenians, Armenian, nazi, people).

The primary limitation of this pipeline is K-means' hard clustering assumption, which forces each document into exactly one topic. Since real-world texts often contain thematic overlap, a soft clustering approach like a Gaussian Mixture Model might better capture nuanced documents. Additionally, the extreme brevity of the corpus texts occasionally yields sparse signals that require carefully calibrated dimension reduction to maintain boundary stability.
